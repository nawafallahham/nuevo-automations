How to track sales from WhatsApp ads and see your real ROAS

Click-to-WhatsApp ads are easy to launch and hard to measure: the sale happens in a chat Meta can't see. Here is how to connect each order back to the ad that started it.

By Nawaf Allahham, founder of Nuevo Automations · Updated 26 November 2026

Ads Manager can't see what happens inside a WhatsApp chat, so for click-to-WhatsApp ads it reports conversations started, not sales. To see real return on ad spend, capture the click ID WhatsApp passes with each chat that starts from an ad, and when that chat turns into an order, send a Purchase event with the order value to Meta's Conversions API. Meta then attributes the sale to the ad and can optimise for buyers instead of chatters.

Why Ads Manager shows conversations, not sales

On a website, the Meta Pixel sees the purchase page. In WhatsApp there's no pixel: the order is agreed in a chat and paid by link or on delivery. Unless you tell Meta, it only knows a conversation started. Brands then judge ads on cost per conversation, and Meta learns to find people who like to chat.

The pieces that connect a chat to an ad

  1. The click ID. When a chat starts from a click-to-WhatsApp ad, the WhatsApp Business API includes a referral with the ad's details and a click ID (ctwa_clid) in the first message your system receives. Store it on the chat.
  2. The order. When the chat becomes a paid or delivered order, record its value and currency against the same chat.
  3. The Conversions API. Send Meta a Purchase event for business messaging, with the click ID, value and currency. Meta matches it to the ad.

Meta documents this as the Conversions API for business messaging. The dataset needs to be linked to your WhatsApp Business account.

Which events to send

  • Lead: the buyer gave real intent, such as asking for a price for a specific product.
  • Initiate checkout: a payment link was sent or a cash-on-delivery order was started.
  • Purchase: the order was paid, or delivered for cash on delivery, with the value.

Send Purchase only once per order, and only when the money is real. Counting placed cash-on-delivery orders that later get refused at the door inflates ROAS.

Reading the numbers honestly

  • Meta's ROAS is an estimate. It depends on the attribution window and modelled conversions. Compare it with your store's own orders each month.
  • Don't credit the ad with every order. Repeat customers who message you directly aren't ad sales.
  • Look per ad, not only per campaign. One creative often carries the account.

Our Khaleej ParaPharma case study shows what this looks like in practice: a 19.6x average return on ad spend across 14 campaigns as Meta estimates it, reported beside the store's own figures. It's one brand's result, not a typical one.

Once sales flow back

With purchases reaching Meta, you can run a Sales objective that optimises for buyers, set cost caps on real orders and drop ads that start chats but never sell. This is built into M2S: each chat is tagged with the ad that started it, and sales made in the chat are reported back to Meta automatically.

Common questions

Can Meta Pixel track WhatsApp sales?

No. The pixel only runs on web pages. Sales made inside WhatsApp chats need to be sent to Meta through the Conversions API, tied to the ad's click ID.

What is ctwa_clid?

It's the click ID the WhatsApp Business API passes with the first message of a chat that started from a click-to-WhatsApp ad. Sending it back with a Purchase event lets Meta attribute the sale to that ad.

Should I optimise click-to-WhatsApp ads for conversations or sales?

For sales, once purchases are reaching Meta. Optimising for conversations teaches Meta to find people who start chats, not people who buy.

Is Meta's reported ROAS accurate?

It's an estimate based on its attribution window and modelling. Compare it every month with your store's own delivered orders.

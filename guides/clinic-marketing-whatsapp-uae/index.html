Clinic marketing and patient messaging on WhatsApp in the UAE

Clinics in the UAE market under stricter rules than almost any other business, and WhatsApp is now part of that marketing. Here is what the health regulators, the health data law and Meta say as of November 2026, and how to run patient enquiries and reminders on WhatsApp within them.

By Nawaf Allahham, founder of Nuevo Automations · Updated 28 November 2026

UAE clinics have three sets of rules to follow. The Dubai Health Authority's standards for medical advertising on social media, re-issued in August 2026, cover WhatsApp alongside Instagram and TikTok, ban words such as "100%", "no side effects", "the best" and "immediate results", require written patient consent for testimonials and images, and allow before-and-after photos only of the same person, unedited, with a results-may-vary statement. MOHAP issues healthcare advertising licences, including one for social media. Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 limits storing or transferring health data outside the UAE, so WhatsApp messages should carry appointment logistics, not clinical details.

General information, not legal advice. Confirm current requirements with your licensing health authority (DHA, DoH or MOHAP) before you advertise.

DHA standards for social media advertising (Dubai)

The DHA re-issued its Standards for Medical Advertisement Content on Social Media in a circular dated 17 August 2026. Its earlier 2022 standard, which press reports of the new circular closely follow, applies to all DHA-licensed facilities and professionals and names WhatsApp among the social media it covers.

  • Banned claims: press reports of the 2026 circular list words such as "unique", "the best", "exclusive", "safest", "the only", "magic", "miraculous", "assured success", "has no side effects", "100%", "absolutely certain" and "immediate results", plus comparisons like "safer than".
  • Before-and-after images: the same person, the same lens, no editing, with a statement that results may vary and the risks, in the same font size as the rest of the ad.
  • Consent: documented written consent from any patient who appears or gives a testimonial, and separate parental consent for anyone under 18.
  • Approval inside the clinic: posts by staff or influencers that name the facility must be approved by the medical director, and the facility keeps an archive for audits.
  • Titles: no job titles that differ from the DHA licence, such as "anti-aging specialist" or "beauty expert".

The penalties described are disciplinary action; we found no published fine amounts for these standards.

Advertising licences

  • MOHAP: the 2022 DHA standard requires a facility's official account to show its medical advertisement licence number from MOHAP. MOHAP's service pages list a licence for healthcare advertising on social media at AED 1,000 a month or AED 3,000 a year plus an AED 100 application fee (from MOHAP's pages as seen in search; confirm before applying).
  • Abu Dhabi: the Department of Health runs its own health media and advertising system, with mandatory training for licensed facilities. We couldn't confirm its fees.
  • Medical products: separately, ads for medical products need Emirates Drug Establishment approval since November 2026, and influencers may not promote them.

Patient data and WhatsApp

Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of ICT in health fields applies across the UAE, free zones included:

  • Health data from services provided in the UAE may only be stored, processed or transferred outside the country in cases allowed by the health authorities (Article 13). Ministerial Resolution No. 51 of 2021 lists those exceptions, several of which require patient consent and a copy kept in the UAE.
  • Using patient data for non-health purposes, such as marketing, needs the patient's written approval (Article 16).
  • Breaching the storage rule carries a fine of AED 500,000 to 700,000 (Article 24).

WhatsApp's own Business Messaging Policy also says not to use WhatsApp to send health information where local rules require stronger protection. The practical approach most clinics take:

  • Keep WhatsApp messages to logistics: appointment date, time, branch, preparation instructions that aren't specific to a diagnosis.
  • Keep results, diagnoses and clinical notes in your UAE-hosted clinic system, not in chats.
  • Collect the patient's opt-in for WhatsApp messages at booking, and a separate one for marketing.

Appointment reminders on WhatsApp

A reminder of an appointment the patient booked fits Meta's definition of a utility message: triggered by the patient, specific to them and non-promotional. Add an offer ("and 20% off fillers this week") and Meta treats the whole message as marketing. Keep reminders and promotions separate. Our AI receptionist page covers booking and reminders in more detail.

Meta's rules for clinic ads

  • No personal health attributes: an ad can't imply it knows the viewer's condition. "Do you have diabetes?" is rejected; "New diabetes treatment available" is allowed.
  • Cosmetic procedures and weight loss: target 18 and over, and avoid content that creates a negative self-image.
  • No health-condition targeting: Meta removed detailed targeting options tied to health conditions in January 2022.
  • Data restrictions for health websites: industry sources report that from January 2025 Meta began restricting the data it accepts from websites it classifies as health and wellness, removing URL details and blocking some lower-funnel events such as Lead, Schedule and Purchase for optimisation outside Europe. This comes from secondary sources rather than a Meta page we could read, so check your data source settings in Events Manager.

Common questions

Can clinics in Dubai advertise on WhatsApp and Instagram?

Yes, within the DHA's standards for medical advertising on social media, which name WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok and others. Claims must be accurate, banned words avoided, and patient images and testimonials need written consent.

Which words are banned in Dubai clinic ads?

Press reports of the DHA's August 2026 circular list words such as "the best", "unique", "safest", "the only", "miraculous", "100%", "no side effects" and "immediate results", along with comparisons like "safer than".

Are before-and-after photos allowed in UAE clinic ads?

Under DHA's standards, only of the same person with the same lens and no editing, with a clear statement that results may vary and the risks. Meta separately restricts some before-and-after content.

Can a clinic send appointment reminders on WhatsApp?

Yes, with the patient's opt-in. Keep reminders to date, time and location; UAE law restricts storing or transferring health data outside the country, so clinical details belong in your UAE-hosted system.

Do clinics need a licence to advertise on social media in the UAE?

MOHAP lists a healthcare advertising licence for social media, and DHA's 2022 standard requires the licence number on the facility's official account. Abu Dhabi's DoH runs its own system. Confirm with your authority.

Advertising supplements in the UAE: Meta's rules and the local ones

Supplement and wellness brands in the UAE answer to two rulebooks: Meta's advertising standards and the UAE's own approval rules. Here is what each says as of November 2026, and how to keep the ad and the WhatsApp chat behind it on the right side of both.

By Nawaf Allahham, founder of Nuevo Automations · Updated 26 November 2026

On Meta, ads for dietary, health and herbal supplements must target people aged 18 or over, can't use before-and-after images or make people feel bad about their bodies, and can't promise a specific result in a set time. In the UAE, the Emirates Drug Establishment said on 18 November 2026 that every advertisement for a medical product needs its approval before it runs, on any channel including social media, and that influencers may not promote medical products. Supplements have their own registration and approval routes, which differ by product and emirate, so check your product's classification with the regulator before you advertise.

This is a plain-language summary for marketing teams, not legal advice. Rules change and depend on how your product is registered. Confirm with the regulator or a regulatory consultant before you spend.

Meta's rules for supplement and wellness ads

Meta's Health and Wellness advertising standards apply everywhere, including the UAE:

  • 18 and over only. Ads for dietary, health, herbal or weight-loss products must be targeted to people aged at least 18.
  • No negative self-image. Ads can't imply or try to create a negative view of someone's body to sell a health product. Before-and-after photos and a person on a scale are Meta's own examples of what's not allowed.
  • No promised outcomes in a timeframe. "Lose 5% of your body weight in a month" is the kind of claim Meta rejects.
  • Prescription products are a different policy. Weight-loss drugs and other prescription medicines fall under Meta's drugs and pharmaceuticals rules, which need written permission and country targeting.

The UAE rules: what changed in November 2026

On 18 November 2026 the Emirates Drug Establishment (EDE) issued a clarification on medical product advertising, reported by The National and Gulf News:

  • Every advertisement for a medical product must be approved by the EDE before it's published, on social media, websites, TV, radio, outdoor screens or print.
  • Social media influencers may not promote medical products, whether in paid posts, sponsored content or endorsements.

Whether your product counts as a "medical product" depends on how it is registered. Supplements, herbal products and cosmetics have their own registration routes, and some emirates add their own approval step. Regulatory consultants report, for example, that Dubai Municipality requires prior approval through its Montaji system for health supplement advertising and promotions.

Separately, anyone in the UAE who advertises on social media for payment needs the UAE Media Council's advertiser permit.

A practical checklist before you launch

  1. Confirm how each product is registered and which authority approves its advertising.
  2. Get the approval, and keep the approved claims written down in English and Arabic.
  3. Build every ad from those approved claims only; the Arabic version says the same thing as the English one.
  4. Set the audience to 18+ in Ads Manager.
  5. Drop before-and-after imagery and time-bound results.
  6. If you work with creators, check they hold the Media Council permit and that the product category allows creator promotion at all.

The WhatsApp chat is part of your marketing too

A click-to-WhatsApp ad can be fully compliant and the sales chat behind it can still overclaim, especially when a busy team or an unchecked chatbot is answering "will this help me lose weight?" at midnight.

When we set up M2S for a wellness brand, the AI assistant answers from the brand's own product sheet and approved claims. It doesn't diagnose or promise results, and it hands medical questions to a person with a summary of the chat. That keeps the conversation honest and in line with what the ad said.

Common questions

Can I target supplement ads to under-18s on Instagram?

No. Meta requires ads for dietary, health and herbal supplements, and weight-loss products, to target people aged 18 or over.

Do supplement ads in the UAE need approval?

Often, yes. Medical product ads need Emirates Drug Establishment approval before they run, and supplements have their own registration and approval routes that can differ by emirate. Check your product's classification with the regulator.

Can influencers promote supplements in the UAE?

Influencers may not promote medical products, under the Emirates Drug Establishment's November 2026 clarification. Whether a supplement falls under that depends on its classification, and paid creators also need the UAE Media Council's advertiser permit.

Are before-and-after photos allowed in Meta wellness ads?

No. Meta lists before-and-after weight-loss photos as an example of content that creates negative self-perception, which its health and wellness standards don't allow.

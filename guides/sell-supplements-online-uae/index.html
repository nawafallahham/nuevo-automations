How to sell supplements online in the UAE

Supplements are one of the fastest-moving online categories in the UAE, and one of the most regulated. This guide pulls together what the official sources say in November 2026, and flags where they leave questions only the regulator can answer.

By Nawaf Allahham, founder of Nuevo Automations · Updated 28 November 2026

To sell supplements online in the UAE you need a trade licence that allows e-commerce (for example Dubai's eTrader or Abu Dhabi's Tajer licence, or a free zone e-commerce licence), and your products must be registered. Federally, the Emirates Drug Establishment (EDE) took over medical product services from the Ministry of Health from 29 December 2025 and lists food supplements under its healthcare product marketing authorisation, which only a licensed medical warehouse can apply for. In Dubai, Dubai Municipality requires health supplements to be registered in its Montaji system before they are imported, advertised or sold. Product details and invoices must be available in Arabic, VAT is 5%, and ads for medical products need EDE approval.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules on supplements changed several times in 2025 and 2026. Confirm your route with the Emirates Drug Establishment (800 33784) and, in Dubai, Dubai Municipality (800900) before you import or advertise.

1. Work out what your product legally is

The same capsule can be a food supplement or a medicine depending on what it contains and what you claim. EDE's healthcare product route covers preparations for general health that are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease, and names food supplements, herbal and homeopathic products among them. A product that makes disease claims, or contains restricted ingredients or medicinal doses, falls into the medicine route, which is far stricter and limits where it can be sold.

Peptides deserve a special warning. In July 2026 EDE acted against 71 sources selling unapproved peptide products for weight loss and referred 14 influencers to the media regulator, according to Gulf News. EDE's position was that calling something a "peptide" doesn't make it an approved product. Treat injectable or therapeutic peptides as medical products, not supplements.

2. Register the products

  • Emirates Drug Establishment (federal). The "Marketing Authorisation for a Healthcare Product" service lists food supplements. The applicant must be an EDE-licensed medical warehouse, the service takes 45 working days, the certificate lasts 5 years, and the application fee is AED 100 (other fees depend on the product). Documents include a free-sale certificate, a halal certificate, a declaration that the product is free of hormones, steroids, heavy metals and pork-derived material, the artwork and the full composition.
  • Dubai Municipality (Dubai). Its technical guidelines for health supplements say no health supplement may be manufactured, imported, advertised, sold or distributed in Dubai unless it is registered in the Montaji system.
  • Abu Dhabi and the other emirates. Local food and health authorities have their own requirements; check with them.

The official pages overlap, and we couldn't confirm from primary sources whether a basic vitamin needs EDE registration, Montaji registration or both. In practice most brands work with a licensed importer or warehouse that handles registration. Ask them which applies to each product, in writing.

3. Get the right licence to sell online

According to the UAE government portal, mainland sellers need a licence with an e-commerce activity from their emirate's economic department, such as Dubai's eTrader licence or Abu Dhabi's Tajer licence, and e-commerce licences also need approval from the TDRA. Free zones issue e-commerce licences too, but selling directly to mainland customers may need a local distributor. Online trade is governed by Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2023.

4. Arabic, labels and claims

  • Under UAE consumer protection law, online sellers must give product details, terms, payment and warranty information in Arabic (other languages can be added), issue invoices in Arabic, and say which authority licenses them.
  • Labels follow the UAE's food supplement, nutrition labelling and health claims standards. Most brands use Arabic and English on pack; confirm with your registration agent.
  • Keep claims to general wellbeing. A claim to treat or prevent a condition can move the product into the medicine route.

5. Advertising: what changed in 2026

  • On 18 November 2026, EDE announced that ads for medical products need its approval before they run, and that social media influencers may not advertise or endorse medical products, as The National reported the next day. The report doesn't say whether supplements registered as healthcare products are included, so ask EDE before you brief creators.
  • Anyone promoting products on social media in the UAE, paid or not, needs an Advertiser Permit, now run by the National Media Authority. Check a creator's permit before you pay them.
  • On Meta, health and wellness ads must be targeted at people 18 or older and avoid before-and-after and "miracle" claims. Our guide to advertising supplements in the UAE covers Meta's rules in detail.

6. Payments, VAT and delivery

  • VAT is 5%. Registration is mandatory above AED 375,000 in taxable sales a year and voluntary from AED 187,500.
  • Card payments: Shopify Payments lists the UAE as a supported country, and Stripe, Network International, Telr, Tabby and Tamara all serve UAE stores. Read your provider's restricted business list: Stripe, for example, prohibits nutraceuticals that are unsafe or make harmful claims.
  • Selling in WhatsApp: many UAE supplement buyers ask questions before paying. Our guide to taking payments on WhatsApp shows how to close those sales in the chat.

Common questions

Do I need to register supplements before selling them online in the UAE?

Yes. In Dubai, Dubai Municipality requires health supplements to be registered in its Montaji system before they are sold or advertised. Federally, the Emirates Drug Establishment lists food supplements under its healthcare product marketing authorisation. Confirm which applies to your product with both.

Who regulates supplements in the UAE now?

Medical product services, including marketing authorisations and advertising approvals, moved from the Ministry of Health and Prevention to the Emirates Drug Establishment from 29 December 2025. Dubai Municipality and other local authorities also register health supplements in their emirates.

Can I sell supplements from a free zone company?

Free zones issue e-commerce licences, but selling directly to mainland UAE customers may need a local distributor or extra approvals, and product registration still applies. Check with your free zone and a registration agent.

Can influencers promote my supplements?

Every creator promoting products in the UAE needs an Advertiser Permit. Since November 2026, influencers may not promote medical products, and ads for them need EDE approval. Ask EDE whether your registered supplement counts before you brief anyone.

Are peptides treated as supplements in the UAE?

No. In July 2026 the Emirates Drug Establishment acted against sellers of unapproved peptide products and said the label "peptide" doesn't make something an approved product. Treat therapeutic peptides as medical products.

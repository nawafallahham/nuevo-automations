# High-converting "book a call" / free audit landing pages: real examples (agencies, chat-commerce vendors, teardowns, call-funnel numbers)

Research date: 2026-11-20. Scope: named, documented examples relevant to a done-for-you service selling an AI WhatsApp sales assistant plus Meta click-to-WhatsApp ads to premium wellness/supplement e-commerce brands, where the page goal is booking a free 30-minute audit call.

Source-quality note: almost every conversion number below is published by the vendor, agency or platform that benefits from it (Unbounce, KlientBoost, Wynter, CRE, Meta, RevenueHero). None is independently audited. Each one is flagged.

## Q1. Performance and e-commerce growth agencies with free audit or strategy call pages: what is on them, and do any publish conversion numbers?

### Takeaway
I found no agency that publishes the conversion rate of its own "book a call" page. What they publish is client results used as proof. The closest analog to Nuevo's offer is Top Growth Marketing's health and wellness page: a supplement/DTC focus, a free audit CTA, a revenue floor, a pricing FAQ, compliance objection handling and a money-back guarantee. Common Thread Collective, the best-known DTC agency, has no audit CTA on its homepage. It sells on brand logos and aggregate dollar claims and routes buyers to a contact page.

### Inferences
- The pattern that recurs on vertical-specific DTC agency pages: vertical-named headline, aggregate scale claim ($ spend managed, number of brands), named-brand results with specific metrics, an explicit revenue floor, published pricing, compliance objection handling for supplements, low-risk terms, then a "free audit" CTA repeated several times.
- For a supplement audience, "claim-safe / ad-rejection" objection handling is a vertical-specific trust lever. Top Growth uses it prominently. An offer that moves conversations into WhatsApp could lean on it too: health claims made in chat still have to be compliant.
- Big agencies with strong brands (CTC) can skip a hard audit CTA. Smaller agencies lean on a free audit as the risk-reducer.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the live audit or contact pages for Disruptive Advertising, Smart Marketer, Foxwell Digital, Pilothouse, KlientBoost (its "free marketing plan" page), Directive or SimpleTiger. No structure or conversion data was captured for them.
- No agency publishes the visit-to-booked-call conversion rate of its own audit page. I found none.

### Inferences
- SaaS demo pages are built for buyers who already know the category. A done-for-you offer sold to supplement founders cannot assume that, so the agency-style long page (Q1) is a better template than the Wati or Respond.io pattern. What is worth borrowing from SaaS: the Meta Business Partner badge, an "AI handles the replies" line next to ads, and "not a group webinar / 1-on-1" framing.
- The strongest proof for "Meta ads into WhatsApp chats" comes from Meta's own success stories (Lamudi, GummiTiles, TENA, Sinharaja). Quoted with the source and the "self-reported" caveat, they give third-party-branded credibility before Nuevo has many case studies of its own.

### Gaps
- Not fetched: Interakt, AiSensy, Manychat, Gorgias, Postscript, Attentive, SleekFlow, Charles and Rasayel demo pages. The Charles URL (hello-charles.com/en/demo) returned 404. No data was collected for these.
- I could not see whether Wati and Respond.io use a calendar embed (e.g. Chili Piper or HubSpot meetings) or a form, because widgets do not render in text fetches.
- No paid-ad-specific landing pages (as opposed to the site's demo page) were found for any chat vendor.

## Q3. Public teardowns and case studies with before/after conversion rates for agency or B2B lead-gen pages

### Takeaway
The best-documented B2B tests (CRE/SFG20 and Wynter/Cognism) point to three levers: reduce the perceived effort of the form, match the page's message to visitor intent, and offer a lower-commitment CTA alongside the call. Most published "lift" numbers come without baselines, sample sizes or test durations, and many bundle in campaign changes.

### Cited Findings
**Conversion Rate Experts: SFG20 multi-step form (+53% leads)**
- **Client:** SFG20, a UK B2B maintenance-spec software company. The test was on its demo-booking form. — [CRE win report](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-multi-step-form-win-report/)
- **Control:** all fields at once (name, email, job title, company, phone).
- **Variant:** first name, last name and company email first. Clicking "Book Your Demo" revealed the remaining fields, with copy explaining why they were needed. No fields were removed.
- **Result:** +53% leads. Duration and significance were not stated. — [same](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-multi-step-form-win-report/)
- **Lessons:**
  - Lean on the Ovsiankina effect: people tend to finish a task they have started.
  - Explain why each extra field is needed.
  - Capture name and email at step 1, so a partial submission still yields a lead. — [same](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-multi-step-form-win-report/)

**Conversion Rate Experts: SFG20 PPC landing page ("a year's worth of PPC leads in 3 weeks")**
- **Diagnosis:** heatmaps showed shallow scrolling and exits through the site menu. Search queries were about the SFG20 standard itself, not the software. — [CRE win report](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-ppc-landing-page-win-report/)
- **Changes:**
  - Headline matched to search intent.
  - A low-commitment CTA (5 resources bundled into one download for an email).
  - One-click demo requests for people who downloaded.
  - Copy rewritten into a "mini customer journey".
  - Stats, logos and visuals added as proof.
  - The site menu replaced with sticky in-page navigation. — [same](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-ppc-landing-page-win-report/)
- **Result:** demo requests in 3 weeks equal to a year's previous volume. No absolute count was given. The variant's users scrolled more than 3x farther (measured where 50% of users stopped scrolling). — [same](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sfg20-ppc-landing-page-win-report/)

**Wynter: Cognism.** Message testing led to a +43% conversion increase on the demo page and +40.22% on the homepage. Vendor-published, with no baselines or sample size. — [Wynter: Cognism case study](https://wynter.com/case-studies/cognism)

**Wynter: Appcues and Databook (vendor claims, seen in search snippets only)**
- Appcues: +73% conversion rate after rewriting copy based on Wynter audience input.
- Databook: website conversions +67% and inbound leads 10x after a messaging relaunch. The page tested was not specified. — [Wynter / Cuspera listing](https://www.cuspera.com/products/wynter-x-18742/customer-story)

**KlientBoost client cases (bundled with PPC changes)**
- Scoro: landing-page redesign plus campaign restructure and remarketing gave conversion rate +24% and CPA −14%; another write-up reports +318% conversion volume. — [KlientBoost Scoro PDF](https://wp.klientboost.com/case-studies/Scoro%20-%20Case%20Study.pdf); [casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/klientboost/case-study/how-scoro-increased-conversion-volume-318)
- SnapLogic: +20% conversions, +11% conversion rate, −10% CPC. — [KlientBoost SnapLogic PDF](https://wp.klientboost.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Snap-Logic-Case-Study.pdf)
- Webconnex: +310% conversion rate and −53% CPA from custom landing pages, A/B tests and SKAGs. — [KlientBoost Webconnex PDF](https://wp.klientboost.com/case-studies/Webconnex%20-%20Case%20Study.pdf)
- Q-See: +72% conversions using a lower-threat lead form with a 15%-off coupon. — [casestudies.com Q-See](https://www.casestudies.com/company/klientboost/case-study/how-a-lead-gen-page-helped-increase-q-sees-roi-on-their-e-commerce-site)
- An individual page test: moving testimonials higher on the page took conversion rate from 12.28% to 23.62%. From KlientBoost's blog, via a search snippet; I did not verify it on the page. — [KlientBoost successful landing pages](https://klientboost.com/landing-pages/successful-landing-pages/)

**Unbounce agency case studies (vendor-published)**
- Workshop Digital: +400% client conversion rate. — [Unbounce: Workshop Digital](https://unbounce.com/customer-case-study/workshop-digital/)
- Effin Amazing: 44% conversion rate. — [Unbounce: Effin Amazing](https://unbounce.com/customer-case-study/effin-amazing)
- Titan PPC: "15%+ conversion rates" for clients. — [casestudies.com Titan PPC](https://www.casestudies.com/company/unbounce/case-study/titan-ppcs-magic-formula-for-high-converting-landing-pages)
- RevUnit / School of Rock: the engagement began with an audit, then PPC iteration. Final numbers were not captured. — [Unbounce: RevUnit](https://unbounce.com/marketing-agencies/revunit-case-study/)
- Webistry (Montreal):
  - Results: Miami Dental Clinics +300% new monthly patients; Utah Limousine +750% monthly bookings.
  - Methods: dedicated landing pages instead of the homepage, message match between ad and page, one CTA per page.
  - No baseline rates; an A/B test is mentioned but its result is not reported. — [Unbounce: Webistry](https://unbounce.com/customer-case-study/webistry-digital-agency/)

**RevenueHero inbound data (vendor-published)**
- Data: over 1M B2B SaaS inbound form submissions from RevenueHero customers over 12 months.
- Qualified-to-booked: median 62%, top 10% at 78%+, best performer 88%. The page says teams at 30–40% are underperforming. — [RevenueHero 2026 benchmarks](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- The page attributes the gap mainly to "the 30 seconds after the form fill", i.e. instant scheduling after submission, with "a 20–30 percentage point advantage". It claims intent decays from about 80% in the first minute to about 40% by the next business day, with no source. — [same](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- Form length barely mattered among top performers: 77% with 2 fields vs 76% with 13 fields. — [same](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- E-commerce vertical: 68.8% qualified-to-booked. — [same](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- CTA wording among top performers: "Book a Demo" 29%, "Get a Demo" 21%, "Schedule a Demo" 12%, "Request a Demo" 12%. — [same](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- Caveats: "qualified" is undefined, the weighting is unexplained, and the year labels are inconsistent (2025 vs 2026). — [same](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)

### Inferences
- For a free 30-minute audit page:
  - Put the calendar inline, or show it right after a short form, so the booking happens in the same session. RevenueHero's data suggests the drop between form and booking is where most of the leakage is.
  - If qualification fields are needed (revenue, store URL, ad spend), use a 2-step form in the SFG20 style rather than removing the fields.
- A secondary low-commitment CTA could catch visitors who are not ready to book, e.g. "see a sample audit" or "chat with the AI assistant on WhatsApp now". This mirrors the SFG20 resource pack, and the WhatsApp version also demos the product.
- Message match matters: one ad angle, one page (Webistry, CRE).

### Gaps
- No Instapage, Leadpages or Swipe Pages customer story with agency "book a call" before/after rates was retrieved.
- No CXL teardown was retrieved.
- Unbounce and KlientBoost numbers mostly lack baselines and are mixed with traffic changes.

## Q4. High-ticket application / strategy-call funnels: documented opt-in to booked-call to show-up rates

### Takeaway
No authoritative public dataset for ClickFunnels-style or Hormozi-style call funnels turned up. What exists is vendor benchmarks and anecdotes:
- Show-up rates of about 60–85%, higher for warm inbound and for calls booked soon after application.
- Qualified-to-booked of about 62% median (RevenueHero, SaaS).
- Gym "set/show/close" figures from Two-Brain.

### Cited Findings
**Show rates**
- One consultancy guide puts show rates at 50–70% for cold-sourced calls and 80%+ for calls booked from a warm application or referral. Seen in a search snippet; the exact source page was not isolated. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/good-conversion-rates-in-sales/)
- A B2B SaaS demo guide claims a median show rate of 62–72% and a top quartile of 78%+. Inbound demo requests show at 75–85%; SDR-booked outbound at 55–65%. The source is an agency selling this service. — [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-demo-show-up-rate-no-show-recovery-benchmarks-2026-by-channel-acv-reminder-cadence.md)
- SegMetrics' sales-call playbook (via a search snippet) says same-day and next-day bookings show 80%+, while bookings more than 5 days out drop below 50%. The PDF itself returned 404, so this is unverified. — [SegMetrics playbook](https://segmetrics.io/wp-content/uploads/Playbook-Sales-Call-Show-Rate.pdf)
- One agency anecdote: show rate rose to 82% within 2 weeks after tightening application questions and adding social proof to the confirmation page. A single unverified case. — [Contra portfolio](https://contra.com/p/XsBRbkke-high-ticket-b2-b-sales-funnel-overhaul)

**Booking and close rates**
- RevenueHero: a median of 62% of qualified inbound requests become booked meetings (see Q3). — [RevenueHero](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)
- Two-Brain Business (gym funnels, Chris Cooper): the Top 10 gyms averaged 40 appointments set, 32 shows and 23 closes per month, about an 80% show rate and about 72% close-on-show. These are high-value local service clients, not B2B. — [Two-Brain: World-Class Funnels](https://twobrainbusiness.com/?p=155502); [podcast: 40 appointments, 32 shows, 23 sales](https://www.buzzsprout.com/46217/episodes/18330679-40-appointments-32-shows-23-sales-world-class-gym-funnels?t=0)
- Hormozi defines show rate as the share of people with an appointment who attend. In one talk he reportedly benchmarked a 27% close rate against about 40% typical for a mid-priced two-call consumer service. Secondhand, via a podcast transcript aggregator. — [Hormozi Ep 598 transcript](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/the-game-with-alex-hormozi/i-doubled-a-business-in-60-days-to-show-its-not-luck-ep-598)

### Inferences
- The figures that most affect show rate, as these sources describe them:
  - Book the call within 24–48 hours of the click: offer only the next few days on the calendar.
  - Confirmation-page proof and a reminder sequence.
  - A warm source: an application or inbound request rather than outbound.
- For a WhatsApp-native offer, confirmations and reminders could be sent over WhatsApp itself. The sources do not cover this, but it is consistent with their emphasis on reminder cadence.
- A reasonable planning model, assembled from the vendor benchmarks above and not a verified benchmark:
  - Page visit to booked: unknown, with no reliable public number for agency audit pages.
  - Booked to show: 65–85%.
  - Show to close: varies widely, about 25–40% in Hormozi's cited figures.

### Gaps
- No ClickFunnels-published data on application-funnel conversion was found.
- No Hormozi or Acquisition.com primary data on opt-in to booked rates was found.
- No reliable public benchmark exists for the visit-to-booked-call rate on agency free-audit pages. This is the key number for Nuevo's page, and it should be measured on Nuevo's own funnel.

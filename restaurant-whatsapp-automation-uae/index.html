Short answer

For restaurants and cafés in the UAE, we run offer and menu ads on Instagram and Facebook that open a WhatsApp chat. M2S, our Message to Sale system, answers in seconds from your own menu, hours and delivery areas, takes delivery and pickup orders with a card payment link or cash on delivery, books tables, collects catering and event enquiries for your manager, and sends repeat-order nudges within WhatsApp's rules. You get a monthly report showing which ads brought the orders.

The problem

Why restaurants miss orders on WhatsApp

A restaurant's WhatsApp is busiest exactly when the team is busiest. At 7:45 PM someone asks if you deliver to their building, someone else wants to know if the biryani is spicy, and a regular wants to order pickup. The host is seating guests and the manager is on the floor. By the time anyone looks, the order is an hour old and the guest has opened a delivery app instead.

The questions themselves are simple and repetitive: are you open, do you deliver here, what's in the family deal, do you have vegan options, can I book the terrace. They need an accurate answer from your menu, not a person. And the guests who order directly are often your best regulars, but without any follow-up they drift away, and nobody can tell which ad actually filled the kitchen.

What it looks like

Menu question to paid order during the rush

The prices and menu in this example are made up; in a real setup they come from your own menu, and the assistant never invents a dish, a price or a substitution you don't offer. It confirmed the delivery area, the swap and the address, then sent a secure card payment link. When the payment came through, the order went to your team with the guest's details, so the kitchen decides timing, not the bot.

If Omar had chosen cash, the assistant would have confirmed a cash-on-delivery order instead. If he'd asked about a serious allergy, a complaint, a large event or anything outside your menu, your manager would get an instant WhatsApp alert with a summary of the chat.

Bringing guests back

Follow-ups and repeat orders, within the rules

Every guest who orders on WhatsApp becomes someone you can reach again, if you do it politely. M2S follows up on payment links that weren't paid, on catering and event enquiries that weren't confirmed, and sends past guests an occasional repeat-order nudge or a new-menu note, timed for lunch or dinner rather than random hours.

Every follow-up stops the moment the guest orders, books or replies. Nothing goes out at night, anyone who says "stop" is never messaged again, and each guest gets at most one sequence a week. M2S doesn't replace delivery apps; it handles the guests who message you directly, and makes sure they come back to you.

FAQ

Common questions from restaurant owners

Does it replace delivery apps?

No. It handles guests who message you directly on WhatsApp, often your regulars, takes their order in the chat and passes it to your team to confirm.

Can guests pay inside the WhatsApp chat?

Yes. Once we connect your payment provider, such as Strabl or Ziina, the assistant sends a secure card payment link with the right amount. Guests can also choose cash on delivery or pay on pickup.

How does it handle allergy questions?

It answers from the menu information you give it, and you choose which questions always go to a person. Serious allergies are passed to your manager with an instant WhatsApp alert.

Can it take reservations and catering enquiries?

Yes. It takes party size, date, time and seating preference for tables, and collects the date, numbers and details for catering and events, then hands those to your manager to confirm.

Will guests get spammed?

No. Follow-ups stop the moment a guest orders or replies, never go out at night, stop for anyone who says stop, and run once per guest per week.

How much does it cost?

M2S is one fixed monthly fee based on your ad budget, your channels and the size of your menu and number of branches, quoted in writing after a free 20-minute audit. Ad spend is paid directly to Meta.
